# Valuations of the second Euler half-block

Let $N=q^a m^2$ be an odd perfect number in Euler form with $a>1$. Put $d=(a+1)/2$, $H=(q+1)/2$, $B=(q^d+1)/2$ and $I=\sigma(m^2)/q^a$. For every odd prime $r\mid H$, the exact relation
$$v_r(B)=v_r(H)+v_r(d)$$
follows from lifting the exponent in $q^d+1$ at odd $d$. If $I=\ell t^2$ for $\ell=1$ or prime, $B$ must be square by the two coprime half-blocks and Hirakawa's geometric-sum theorem. Hence $v_r(H)$ and $v_r(d)$ have the same parity for every $r\mid H$. In particular, every prime to odd valuation in $H$ must divide $d$. The first equality holds more generally for every odd natural base and odd length under the displayed odd-prime divisibility condition. [Exact valuation and index consequences](../OddPerfect/HalfBlockValuation.lean) · [Lifting the exponent in mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Multiplicity.lean)

If 3 or 5 occurs to odd valuation in $H$, the corresponding prime must divide $d$. Either possibility contradicts the checked cubic or quintic nonsquare theorem for $B$. For example, when $q=113$, $H=57=3\cdot19$ so all Euler exponents greater than one exclude a prime times a square in the index. The argument has no upper bound on $q,a$ or $N$. Run `lake build OddPerfect.HalfBlockValuation` to replay it. [Cubic and quintic proofs](EULER_SQUARE_CLASS.md)

## Prime lengths seven, eleven and thirteen

For $s=7,11,13$, the alternating integer $C_s(x)=(x^s+1)/(x+1)$ is nonsquare at every natural $x>1$. The proofs place an even multiple of its prospective square root strictly between consecutive even integers. The degree-$3,5,6$ approximations work respectively for $x\ge4,57,214$. Smaller inputs are evaluated in the Lean kernel. At length seven the central odd integer is $16x^3-8x^2+6x-5$ and the scale is $16$. The other two polynomials and their positivity certificates appear in the proof. These are individual instances of Ljunggren's geometric-square classification at a negative base. [Square intervals](../OddPerfect/SmallOddLengths.lean) · [Ljunggren, Theorem 1](https://msleziak.com/temp/Ljunggren.pdf)

If $x\equiv1\pmod{16}$, then $C_s(x)\equiv1\pmod8$, and its only possible prime divisor shared with $(x+1)/2$ is $s$. Since each of these three primes differs from one modulo eight, $C_s(x)$ is neither a square nor $s$ times a square. At other Euler bases the residue obstruction to $(x^s+1)/2$ already applies. Replacing $x$ by $q^{d/s}$ gives the exclusion for every $d$ divisible by any of $7,11,13$, in particular for Euler exponents $a=13,21,25$. Thus $I$ can be neither a square nor a prime times a square throughout these cases. Run `lake build OddPerfect.SmallOddLengths` on the project pin.
