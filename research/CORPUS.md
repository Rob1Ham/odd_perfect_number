# Perfect-number literature and Lean coverage

Let σ sum the positive divisors, ω count distinct prime divisors, and Ω count them with multiplicity. In the odd-perfect rows, N is an odd natural number satisfying mathlib's `Nat.Perfect`. An exact component is a prime power with the full exponent at that prime. The status beside each result concerns this checkout. The last column identifies the mathematical or computational source.

## Using the corpus

A published inequality can guide a choice of branch. To use it inside a proof, import a checked declaration with the required hypotheses or prove it locally. For a computation-assisted exclusion, record the parent predicate, every child predicate, a coverage theorem for the children, and a certificate checked at every terminal node. A partial factorization can certify a divisor by multiplication or a remainder check. A claim that no other prime can occur requires a further proof.

The corpus is a working bibliography. For larger results without a local declaration, the coverage column records the outstanding proof work. The audit and case ledger describe what the local project can currently use. [Case ledger](LEDGER.md)

## Additional checked consequences

The absolute and linear Ochem–Rao count bounds together give Ω≥2ω+5 for every odd perfect number and Ω≥2ω+7 when 3 is absent. The stronger published additive bound requires its own factor chains. [Declarations and published comparison](COUNT_CHAIN.md)

For four-prime deficient-perfect cofactors without 5 or 7 the complementary divisor equals three. A Fermat prime dividing the divisor sum of a square requires a source and a distinct primitive target, both one modulo that prime. Their membership in the cofactor follows from the exact equation 3σ(n)=5n. This supplies an exclusion covering an omitted prime in the enumeration of Yang and Togbé’s §5.3. [Sources, statements and coverage](DEFICIENT_PERFECT.md) · [Order and valuation proofs](DEFICIENT_FERMAT.md)

At a component whose index is an even power of a prime, two congruences for the radical give a quadratic bound for the base in terms of its exponent. [Exact hypotheses and congruences](EVEN_INDEX_RADICAL_LIFTS.md)

Dutta and Saikia’s Conjecture 3.2 asks for finiteness of odd deficient-perfect n at fixed distinct-prime count. Adajar stated both odd finiteness results in his 2019 conference abstract.
