# Odd perfect numbers: strategy review, 27 November 2026

The public library can exclude specific families at arbitrary size. The argument for excluding **all** odd perfect numbers is missing in particular at two places in the case tree. At Euler exponent one, a candidate can have a prime sixth power and many interacting components. At exponent greater than one, its Euler index can have two or more primes of odd valuation. Neither case has a contradiction or a uniform bound with a discharged finite remainder. Even a complete proof for sixth-power-free numbers would only force a prime sixth power in every candidate. [Case tree](BRANCH_MAP.md) · [Target](../OddPerfect/Question.lean)

The review produced a kernel-checked exclusion. For a sixth-power-free candidate whose exponent-two primes are all 1 modulo 5, the number of those primes must be strictly less than twice the number of exponent-four primes that are 1 modulo 3. The proof and its companion inequality are now in the public import graph. This is a newly tracked slice motivated by an existing unbounded family. Its closure provides no numerical measure of distance to the full conjecture. The final full audit passed for 10,090 named theorems and 60,981 project declarations across 921 reachable modules. ## A direction that arithmetic distinguishes

On the sixth-power-free branch let $S$ be the number of primes of exact exponent two, $U$ the number of these outside $1\bmod5$, and $Q$ the number of exponent-four primes congruent to $1\bmod3$. The public [Lean module](../OddPerfect/SquareOutputCapacity.lean) proves the inequality

$$
10S\le 19Q+5U+10.
$$

1. Every square source $s$ is $2\bmod3$, and each prime factor of $F_s=s^2+s+1$ belongs to the set $T$ of those $Q$ quartic primes together with the Euler prime. Indeed $3\nmid N$, and a target different from 3 has order three for the base modulo that target. A target congruent to one modulo three cannot itself be a square source. Put $X=Q+1$. The total multiplicity $L=\sum_s\Omega(F_s)$ is at most $4Q+1=4X-3$ by the incoming valuations, including the Euler prime's single slot. [Bipartite accounting](../OddPerfect/SixthPowerFreeBipartiteParity.lean)
2. Let $P,B$ count the sources whose outputs have one and two prime factors with multiplicity, respectively. Each of these outputs has a factor $t>s$, since $F_s>s^2$. Two different eligible sources below the same $t$ cannot both be roots. Their sum would be $t-1$, yet the sources are both $2\bmod3$ and $t\equiv1\bmod3$. Thus $P+B\le X$. Counting the remaining sources at weight at least three gives $L\ge3S-2P-B$, hence $P\ge3S-5X+3$. [Vieta root count](../OddPerfect/ZelinskyFullSlope.lean) · [Larger semiprime factor](../OddPerfect/CyclotomicRootSlope.lean)
3. At least $P-U$ distinct targets are prime outputs from $s\equiv1\pmod5$, hence congruent to three modulo five. The other residue classes have at most $4(X-P+U)$ incoming slots together. Call a composite output exceptional if its source is outside $1\bmod5$ or it uses a target outside $3\bmod5$. Their count $D$ satisfies $D\le4(X-P)+5U$. This charges one actual incoming factor per exceptional output with a good source. It allows several sources to use the same prime.
4. Every remaining composite output is $3\bmod5$ and all its factors are $3\bmod5$. As 3 has order four modulo five, its multiplicity is $1\bmod4$, hence at least five. All semiprime outputs are exceptional. Therefore $L\ge5S-4P-2D-B\ge5S+5P-9X-10U$. Substituting step 2 and $L\le4X-3$ gives the displayed inequality.

The lever is the residue of the **product of all factors of one output** together with the **total available valuations on actual primes**. A bound on the number of residue classes alone permits unlimited representatives. The local reuse example at 73 remains compatible with the argument. Setting $U=0$ and $S\ge2Q$ forces $Q\le10$. The existing theorem $Q\ge80$ therefore closes this entire slice. The public LP baseline includes the modulo-five row. The theorem uses the Euler prime furnished by Euler form and takes no auxiliary counting hypothesis. [Exclusion](../OddPerfect/SquareOutputCapacity.lean) · [Lower bound on Q](../OddPerfect/SixthPowerFreeQuarticCollisionPinch.lean)

## Where to spend the next round

**Restricted experiment.** On the third row, retain separate counts for the residues of targets belonging to actual prime outputs, and for the three contributing sources at a target receiving a $2+1+1$ partition. Test whether those local partitions and their source products can be realized simultaneously. The first deliverable is an incompatible inequality or an explicit feasible assignment to the enlarged model. A fixed offset cannot remove an asymptotic direction. The Fletcher–Nielsen–Ochem least-prime interval bounds only the smallest prime. Their paper explicitly discusses the difficulty of proving termination of alternating factor chains. [Fletcher–Nielsen–Ochem, §§10–12](https://www.lirmm.fr/~ochem/opn/OPNS_Adam_Pace.pdf)

**Unrestricted experiment.** Test the same accounting when targets have arbitrary exponents. Select only exact-square sources $s\equiv2\bmod3$, so their targets still lie among the support primes $t\equiv1\bmod3$. Let $X$ now count those targets, $V=\sum_t v_t(N)$, and $H=\sum_{t\not\equiv3\bmod5}\max(v_t(N)-4,0)$ over this target set. The same calculation gives the paper bound $20S\le14X+6V+10U+2H$: replace the total budget by $V$, and bound exceptional-target capacity by $4(X-P+U)+H$. This identifies exactly what breaks outside the sixth-power-free slice. Seek a compensating compulsory contribution from the high-exponent components themselves. Test an ordinary exponent $e=\ell-1$ with $\ell$ prime, where only one nontrivial cyclotomic block is forced by divisors of the length. Also test candidates with few or no selected square sources, where the displayed inequality is weak or vacuous. A route to the root must address those cases. A result for arbitrary rank on a special slice can stand as its own theorem.

**Decision.** Keep the restricted calculation and the unrestricted test active. Reopen the coprime descent when there is a smaller triple with all the original hypotheses, and the prime-power exponent route when there are explicit, incompatible growth rates. If the reported private Lean proof becomes available, checking its exact theorem and dependencies takes priority over independent construction. There is no mathematical basis in the repository for assigning a percentage of the main conjecture completed.
