# Every prime-power component index has exponent at least 66

Let $N=q^a b$ be odd and perfect, with $q$ prime, $a>0$ and $q\nmid b$. Its exact component index is $i=\sigma(b)/q^a$. If $i=p^j$ for a prime $p$ and a positive integer $j$, then $j\ge66$. The theorem applies at every exact component and permits every index prime. The separate odd-exponent argument already gives $j\ge191$ when $j$ is odd. ## Cofactor sizes for even exponents

For $j=2u$, the previous reduction gives $a=1$, $N=q(p^us)^2$, $q=\sigma(p^{2u})$, $q+1=2s^2$ and $\sigma(s^2)=p^{2u}$. At each prime $r\mid s$, write $\sigma(r^{2v_r(s)})=p^{\beta_r}$. These exponents are positive and odd, sum to $2u$, and the number of primes of $s$ is even. A source with $\beta_r=1$ gives $p\equiv1\pmod r$ and $q\equiv2u+1\equiv-1\pmod r$, so $r\mid u+1$. The other sources cost at least three copies of $p$. Accordingly $3\omega(s)\le 2u+2\omega(u+1)$. [Source identities](EVEN_PRIME_POWER_SOURCES.md) · [Single-copy bound](../OddPerfect/EvenIndexSingleCopy.lean)

When $p\equiv1\pmod4$ we have $16\mid 2u$. When $p\equiv3\pmod4$, all primes dividing $s$ are $1\pmod4$. Suppose $2u<64$. Primality of $2u+1$ gives $2u\le60$. Every prime divisor of $u+1$ is among the eleven primes below 32, so $\omega(s)+1\le28$. The formal comparison uses the slightly larger set of the first 31 primes congruent to 1 modulo 4, through 317. The Euler prime also belongs to this residue class. For $p\ge19$, the upper bound on $\sigma(N)/N$ from this product, multiplied by $19/18$, is strictly below two. For $p=3,7,11$, the checked expression $(\sigma(p^{2u})+1)/2$ lies strictly between two consecutive squares in each admissible case. Fixed Newton iterations provide the candidates for the lower adjacent square, and both inequalities are checked in the kernel. Thus $2u\ge64$ in this residue class. ## The exponent sixteen

Primality of $2u+1$ and the preceding alternatives leave only $j=16$ below 66. Here each cofactor prime contributes a residue $x=p\bmod r$ with $x^{\beta_r}=1$ and $x^{17}+x=2$. If $\beta_r=1$, then $x=1$ and $r\mid18$, forcing $r=3$. If $\beta_r=3$ or $5$, reducing 17 modulo that exponent yields $x^2+x=2$, so $x=1$ or $x=-2$. The latter yields $r\mid9$ or $r\mid33$, respectively. Only 3 can send fewer than five copies, and only 3 and 11 can send fewer than seven. Since $\omega(N)=2+\omega(s)$, the checked bound $\omega(N)\ge5$ and even parity of $\omega(s)$ give at least four distinct sources. Their combined contribution is at least $1+5+7+7=20$, exceeding 16. [Root equations](../OddPerfect/EvenIndexRoots.lean) · [Sixteenth-power exclusion](../OddPerfect/EvenPrimePowerSixteen.lean)
