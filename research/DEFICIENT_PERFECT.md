# Deficient-perfect cofactors and odd spoofs

Let σ(n) denote the actual sum of all positive divisors. A deficient-perfect integer n has a proper divisor d satisfying σ(n)+d=2n. Tóth studies odd positive n for which a positive integer x satisfies 2n/σ(n)−1=1/x. Clearing denominators gives σ(n)(x+1)=2nx. In this report the nontrivial odd multipliers have x>1 and x odd. [Tang and Feng, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972714000082) · [Tóth, 2021, §I](https://cmst.eu/wp-content/uploads/files/10.12921_cmst.2021.0000005_TOTH.pdf)

## Equality of candidate sets

For every odd n, a proper deficient divisor exists if and only if n admits a nontrivial odd multiplier. To recover the deficient divisor from such a multiplier, write x=2t−1. The balance equation gives n=t(2n−σ(n)). Since t>1, the deficiency d=2n−σ(n) is a positive proper divisor. In the reverse direction set t=n/d and x=2t−1. The equation σ(n)+d=2n gives the required balance. The identity σ(n)=xd recovers d directly from n and x. For x=1 the sole pair is (1,1). Descartes's cofactor n=9,018,009 has deficient divisor d=819 and multiplier x=22,021. [Definitions, equivalence, and endpoints](../OddPerfect/DeficientPerfect.lean)

The true prime-power components of n give ω(n) positive odd formal entries. Adding the single entry (x,1) produces a formally balanced list of length ω(n)+1. This construction allows x to be composite or to share prime factors with n. Every such list has length at least five. Consequently every odd deficient-perfect number has at least four distinct prime divisors. This recovers Tang and Feng's exclusion of exactly three, and also covers every smaller support size. [Formal reduction](../OddPerfect/DeficientSupport.lean) · [Minimum length](../OddPerfect/SpoofBelowFive.lean) · [Published three-prime result](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972714000082)

At Euler exponent one, every odd perfect N=q m² gives a pair (m²,q) because q is a prime coprime to m. Thus its Euler index σ(m²)/q is exactly the deficient divisor of m². In the reverse direction, when n and x are coprime and x>1, n x is perfect precisely when x is prime. The identities make it possible to transfer a classification of deficient-perfect cofactors into a restriction on the linear Euler component. ## Four distinct primes

Yang and Togbé proved that the only odd deficient-perfect integer with four distinct prime factors is 3²·7²·11²·13², with deficient divisor 3²·7·13. Their proof first forces 3 into the support and bounds the second prime by 23. These first two reductions and the general lower bound σ(n)/n ≥ 2−1/b when every prime divisor is at least b are checked. The rest of the paper treats the remaining primes and exponents. [Four-prime reductions](../OddPerfect/DeficientFour.lean) The full four-prime classification is a separate formalization target. In particular, its unrestricted conclusion about four distinct primes does not follow from testing n up to a size limit. [Yang and Togbé, 2023, Theorem 1 and §2](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/x5/x5.pdf)
