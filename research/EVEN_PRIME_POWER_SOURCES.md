# Prime-power indices at Euler exponent one

Suppose an exact component of an odd perfect number has index $p^{2u}$, for a prime $p$ and $u>0$. Chen and Chen's theorem in this project gives an Euler component $q$ of exponent one and $q=\sigma(p^{2u})$. Write $m=p^u s$ with $p\nmid s$. Cancelling $p^{2u}$ from $(q+1)p^{2u}=2m^2$ gives $q+1=2s^2$. Cancelling $q=\sigma(p^{2u})$ from $\sigma(m^2)=qp^{2u}$ gives $\sigma(s^2)=p^{2u}$. In particular $q\equiv1\pmod{16}$. [Chen and Chen’s index result](CHEN_CHEN_INDEX.md) · [Cofactor decomposition](../OddPerfect/EvenPrimePowerSources.lean) · [Residues](../OddPerfect/EvenPrimePowerParity.lean)

For each prime $r\mid s$, put $e_r=2v_r(s)$. There is a positive exponent $\beta_r$ such that $\sigma(r^{e_r})=p^{\beta_r}$. If $\beta_r$ were even, Ljunggren's complete geometric-square classification at odd length would force $r=3$, $e_r=4$ and $p=11$. But $\sigma(11^{2u})\equiv1\pmod3$, whereas $3\mid s$ would imply $q\equiv-1\pmod3$. Hence every $\beta_r$ is odd and $\sum_{r\mid s}\beta_r=2u$. The number of distinct prime factors of $s$ is even and at most $2u$. Since $q,p$ are distinct from one another and from all the primes of $s$, $\omega(N)=\omega(s)+2$ is also even. [Geometric-square classification](ODD_GEOMETRIC_SQUARES.md) · [Odd contributions](../OddPerfect/EvenPrimePowerSources.lean) · [Counting their sum](../OddPerfect/EvenPrimePowerParity.lean)

When $p\equiv3\pmod4$, every prime of $s$ is one modulo four. Indeed, if $r\equiv3\pmod4$ then $\sigma(r^{e_r})\equiv1\pmod4$, whereas $p^{\beta_r}\equiv3\pmod4$. When $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the congruence $q\equiv1\pmod{16}$ forces $16\mid2u$. In the identity $q-1=p(p+1)\sum_{k<u}(p^2)^k$, the integer $p(p+1)/2$ is odd, and the last sum is $u$ modulo eight.
