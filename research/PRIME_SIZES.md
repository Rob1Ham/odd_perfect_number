# Upper bounds on prime divisors

For every actual prime divisor q of an odd perfect N, the project proves q³ < 3N, the theorem of Acquaah and Konyagin. The proof applies to every q, so selecting the largest gives the usual formulation. [Acquaah–Konyagin, International Journal of Number Theory 8 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793042112500935) · [Lean declaration](../OddPerfect/AcquaahKonyagin.lean)

If q has exponent at least two, an incoming edge into the singleton {q} suffices. If its exponent is one, choose an incoming component R = pᵉ with σ(R) = qu. Let z be the product of all divisor sums except those at p and q. Then (q+1)quz = 2N and R divides (q+1)z, because R is coprime to its own divisor sum. We claim R ≤ u²z. When p does not divide q+1, R divides z. Otherwise qu ≡ 1 mod p and q ≡ −1 mod p imply p−1 ≤ u. The geometric-sum identity gives R ∣ ((p−1)u−1)z. The multiplier (p−1)u−1 is positive and at most u². This proves the claim in both cases. Finally 2qu = 2σ(R) < 3R, giving 2q < 3uz. Multiplication by q² and the full σ identity give the cubic bound.

For any nonempty set S of j support primes all at least t > 0, `prime_rank_bound` proves 2t^(2j) < 3N. In particular, choose the j largest prime divisors. This refines the coefficient in the general rank estimate of Bibby, Vyncke and Zelinsky's Theorem 2. The second-largest prime has a stronger exponent-specific bound in Zelinsky's earlier work. [Bibby–Vyncke–Zelinsky (2021)](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/v115/v115.pdf) · [Zelinsky](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11734) · [Lean declaration](../OddPerfect/RankBounds.lean)

For proper S the graph gives an outside prime power R with t ≤ σ(R) < 3R/2. Euler's parity restriction makes the sum of the exponents in S at least 2j−1. For the full support, Steuerwald's theorem gives Ω ≥ 2j+1 without an outside component. These cases also cover a singleton and all endpoint ties. An independent worker checked the two proofs and their hypotheses.
