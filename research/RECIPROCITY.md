# Quadratic reciprocity and divisor-sum edges

A prime $r$ dividing $\sigma(p^e)$ at even $e$ sees $p$ as a quadratic residue. Indeed $p^{e+1}=1\pmod r$, so $p$ equals the square of $p^{e/2+1}$ modulo $r$. For distinct prime bases and a target $r\equiv3\pmod4$, the converse also holds when the positive even exponent may be chosen freely. When $p\equiv1\pmod r$, choose $e=r-1$. Otherwise Euler's criterion gives $p^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}=1\pmod r$. The odd length $3\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ supplies a positive even exponent and a geometric sum divisible by $r$. [Lean proofs](../OddPerfect/Reciprocity.lean)

Quadratic reciprocity now determines a unique possible direction between every pair of distinct primes both congruent to 3 modulo 4. In an odd perfect number, Euler's theorem makes both actual exponents even. An observed edge between such primes therefore excludes the reverse edge. The actual exponents can also fail to realize either direction. The project uses mathlib's `ZMod.exists_sq_eq_prime_iff_of_mod_four_eq_three` on the pinned revision. [Mathlib quadratic reciprocity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LegendreSymbol/QuadraticReciprocity.lean)

A separate obstruction applies to an Euler prime $q\equiv5\pmod8$. Its odd factor $(q+1)/2$ is 3 modulo 4 and has a prime divisor $r\equiv3\pmod4$. This $r$ divides the Euler component sum. Since $q\equiv-1\pmod r$, reciprocity makes $r$ a quadratic nonresidue modulo $q$. No even exponent of $r$ can send an edge back to $q$. The cut isolating $q$ also supplies an incoming prime with even exponent, a quadratic residue modulo $q$. Consequently both quadratic character classes occur among distinct prime divisors of the square part.

The local identities $\sigma(3^4)=121$, $\sigma(11^2)=133$, and $\sigma(7^2)=57$ give the directed cycle $3\to11\to7\to3$ using three primes 3 modulo 4. Their product of exact components is $480249$, whose divisor sum is $917301$, below $960498=2\cdot480249$. These identities demonstrate that longer local cycles remain possible without yielding an odd perfect number. The other residue classes also admit two-way edges, as $13\mid\sigma(3^2)$ and $3\mid\sigma(13^2)$.
